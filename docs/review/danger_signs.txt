Ibà self-report questions (the public, through Magana): exact English wording
Source: iba-natlas app/docs/CLINICAL_REVIEW.md item 7, wording in app/backend/app/questions.py
Status: pending clinical sign-off by Dr. Oluba Amos

1. Do you have a fever, or does your body feel very hot?
2. For how many days have you had the fever? (asked if the answer to fever is yes; answer: number of days)
3. Have you had fits, or shaking of the body you could not stop?
4. Are you unable to drink anything, or is the baby unable to breastfeed?
5. Are you vomiting everything you eat or drink?
6. Are you very sleepy, confused, or hard to wake?
7. Are you bleeding without a clear reason, from the nose or gums, or in vomit or stool?
8. Have your eyes or skin turned yellow?
9. Are you breathing very fast, or struggling to breathe?
10. Are your palms, lips or inner eyelids very pale?
11. Is your urine very dark, like cola or tea?
12. Are you too weak to sit up or stand without help?

Rules (item 7): any of questions 3 to 12 answered yes -> emergency: go to the nearest clinic now, or call 112. Fever for 2 days or more with no danger sign -> urgent: a nurse calls back within 2 hours. Otherwise -> routine: a nurse calls back during working hours.

Magana's versions for blind users, asked instead of the sight-based questions (DRAFT, also for Dr. Amos's sign-off; Magana app/lib/l10n/app_en.arb):
1. (bleeding) Have you felt or tasted blood, or has anyone told you that you are bleeding, from your nose, gums, vomit or stool?
2. (yellow_eyes) Has anyone told you that your eyes or skin look yellow?
3. (very_pale) Has anyone told you that you look very pale? Do you feel very weak, dizzy or out of breath when you stand?
4. (dark_urine) Has someone near you seen that your urine is very dark, like cola or tea?

Every question in Magana also offers "I can't tell": never counted as no; on a danger sign it raises priority to at least urgent and Magana says: "Please ask someone near you to check, or go to a clinic."
